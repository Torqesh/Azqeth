# Azqeth

> Copper rain falls upward through the ribs of an unfinished sun.

## The Foundry Before Morning

The foundry begins work before the town remembers waking. Its chimney releases no smoke. Instead, a narrow column of copper rain rises from the roof and disappears above the clouds. By noon, the gutters are dry, though the workers' sleeves remain damp until evening.

The master founder keeps a circular mold beneath his bench. Its center is empty, and its rim contains thirteen impressions shaped like fingers. No hand fits them. The apprentices are forbidden to clean the mold, even when a little white moss appears along its edge.

Each morning, the master pours one spoonful of molten copper into the circle. Each evening, he removes a spoonful of cold water. The mold never seems more complete.

Outside, the town's sundial has no shadow until the foundry doors close.

## The Apprentice Who Brought a Nail

An apprentice arrived carrying a nail he had found inside a loaf of bread. It was copper, warm, and bent into a curve too regular to have been accidental. The baker denied using metal in his oven and asked whether the loaf had been cut from the left.

The apprentice could not remember. He placed the nail beside the circular mold. Its thirteen impressions filled with a little light, and the white moss folded inward like fingers closing around something delicate.

The master sent everyone home except the new apprentice. He opened a cupboard containing a hammer without a head, a pair of tongs holding nothing, and a little stone bowl filled with the reflection of a sunset.

He asked which tool the nail required. The apprentice chose the empty tongs. Something invisible shifted between their jaws, and the copper rain above the chimney stopped rising.

## A Sunset Held Below the Floor

The master placed the stone bowl on the floor. Its reflected sunset spread beyond the rim, revealing a staircase beneath the boards. At its foot lay a field of dark grass, and at the field's center stood a furnace shaped like an open rib cage.

The apprentice carried the tongs down. Their invisible burden grew warmer with every step. Behind him, the master held the bent nail against the bowl's reflected horizon.

Inside the rib-shaped furnace sat a figure made of clear glass. Its hands rested on its knees. Through its chest, the apprentice could see a small copper sun with one piece missing from its edge.

He tried to speak, but the field received his breath as rain. Each drop rose past his face and hung beneath the staircase, waiting for a direction.

## The Sleeper's Unfinished Light

The ribs belonged to a dragon sleeping beneath the town. Its body had become the metal worked in the foundry. Its breath had become the heat of the ovens. Only its sun remained unfinished, held inside the glass figure where a heart might have rested.

The master had found the mold when he was an apprentice himself. He had been told to fill it one spoonful at a time until the missing light returned. Years passed. The town grew around the chimney. The sun inside the figure remained incomplete.

Now the invisible object in the tongs began to show an edge. It resembled a little piece of daylight, bent along the same curve as the nail. The apprentice understood why nothing had seemed to grow in the mold: the shape had been arriving from another side.

Above the field, the hanging drops gathered around the staircase like small suspended bells.

## Thirteen Impressions, Four Marks

Along the copper nail appeared four groups of letters:

*Az — qeth — ul — vor.*

The master read them toward the reflected sunset. The apprentice set the tongs inside the rib cage. Their burden passed through the glass figure's chest and settled into the missing edge of its copper sun.

The nail straightened. The hanging rain fell upward again, rushing through the staircase toward the chimney. For one moment, the dragon's ribs cast the shadows of thirteen hands across the field.

The glass figure lifted its head. It had no face, only a small round window through which the apprentice could see the foundry bench and the circular mold beneath it.

He saw himself entering the workshop for the first time, carrying a nail inside a piece of bread. Before he could move closer, the window filled with ordinary fire.

## The Spoon Returned Empty

At morning, the master found the apprentice beside the closed cupboard. The tongs lay across his knees. Between their jaws rested a little white root, cool and folded around a grain of copper.

The mold beneath the bench was empty. Its thirteen impressions had become smooth. The master poured his usual spoonful into it and, for the first time, heard metal meet metal.

No copper rain rose from the chimney that day. The town's sundial kept its shadow until sunset, and the baker discovered a round hollow in a loaf that had not yet entered his oven.

The apprentice stayed at the foundry. He kept the white root in the stone bowl and turned it once each evening, careful never to count its folds.

Deep beneath the floor, a furnace of ribs holds a completed sun. Its glass attendant remains seated, watching a little window where morning has learned to arrive without losing any of its light.
